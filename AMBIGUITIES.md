# Ambiguities

1. **Backdated value dates (E7: booked Day 5, value_date Day 2).** The money legally left on Day 2 but the ledger only learns of it on Day 5, so past days' closing balances must be re-evaluated. Resolution: closing balance for any day D = sum of entries with value_date <= D, recomputed after each new event; overdraft fees are assessed point-in-time / as-known (when the overdraft becomes known), not on a hypothetical final balance — criterion 1's "evaluated at end of Day 5" confirms this reading. (Which days then take a fee is ruled in REJECTED, criterion 2.)

2. **E10 is listed after E9 but is a Day-5 event (E9 is Day 6).** If "end of Day 5" processing fires the moment a Day-6 event is seen, E10 is missed and ACC-002's Day-5 closing balance (and its interest) is wrong. Resolution: order events by (booking day, then stream order) and run end-of-day processing per booking day, so E10 is folded into Day 5 before Day 5 closes. This reaches the same end state as replaying strictly in the listed order and re-opening Day 5 when E10 (a Day-5 event) appears after E9 — the booking-day sort is an implementation convenience, not a change to the required "replayed in this order" semantics.

3. **Available-balance basis for an authorization check.** Does "ledger balance" in the auth rule mean all booked entries, or only entries with value_date <= today? Resolution: use all booked entries minus active holds — a backdated debit still means the money is gone now. (Same result on this stream; recorded because it changes behaviour on other inputs.)

4. **Partial settlement releases the whole hold.** Auth-A settles 185 against a 200 hold (E5). Resolution: a settlement releases the **entire** authorization hold, not just the settled portion — standard card-scheme behaviour — so no residual 15 hold lingers. This is load-bearing for the Auth-B decline: available at E8 = ledger −155 − 0 active holds = −155 (it would be −170 if 15 of Auth-A's hold survived), which is why criterion 5's antecedent never fires.

5. **Overdraft-fee currency for a BHD account.** The fee is stated as "AED 25.00", but the rule is per account and ACC-002 is BHD. Resolution: the fee amount is currency-specific and the brief defines only the AED figure; ACC-002 never closes negative in this window, so no BHD fee is defined or assessed. A production engine would need a per-currency fee schedule; here the case is unreachable, but the engine's stance is that a fee's currency must match the account's currency.

6. **value_date of a retroactively-assessed overdraft fee — the negative day, not the discovery day.** The rule says the fee is "booked with value_date equal to the day assessed," and the preceding clause defines the trigger as "that day's closing ledger balance … is negative." Reading "the day assessed" as *the day whose balance was assessed-as-negative*, the fee's value_date is the negative day itself (Day 2, Day 4, Day 5), not the Day 5 on which E7 makes them known. Criterion 1's "the Day 2 closing ledger balance … **before any fee is assessed**, is −370.00" is direct textual support: it frames Day 2's balance around a fee that attaches to Day 2. **Consequence:** fees compound — a Day-2-dated fee (value_date Day 2) is included in the closing balance of every day D ≥ 2. On this stream the fee-inclusive closings are Day 2 = −395, Day 3 = +5, Day 4 = −205, Day 5 = −230 (fee-days 2, 4, 5). A fee never re-triggers its own day (once per day per account); it can, in general, push a *later* day negative and that later day then takes its own single fee (does not occur here — Day 3 stays positive at +5).

7. **Interest is accrued as-known (point-in-time), not recomputed at capitalization.** "Daily interest: 0.04% per day on the closing ledger balance, positive balances only. Accruals capitalize as a single credit at end of Day 6." The ambiguity is *which* closing balance a backdated event (E7, E9) implies for a past day. Ruling: each day's accrual is computed on that day's closing balance **as known when that day closes** (events processed in booking-day order) and is **immutable** thereafter — a later backdated entry does not restate a past accrual. Support: (a) "The ledger is append-only. No event record is ever mutated or deleted" — recomputing a booked accrual on a later pass is a mutation; (b) criterion 1 evaluates a balance "at end of Day 5," i.e. the brief's own evaluation is point-in-time; (c) it matches the fee model, which criteria 1/2 force to as-known. **Consequences on this stream:** Day 5 accrues **0** (its closing is −155 at end of Day 5, once E7 is known); Day 2 both earns interest (accrued end of Day 2 on +250) **and** later takes a −25 overdraft fee (assessed end of Day 5 when E7 posts) — not a contradiction: interest was earned while the day closed positive, and the overdraft only appeared afterwards, exactly as nightly accrual behaves against a backdated debit. This also **dissolves the same-day fee/interest ordering question**: at close a day is either positive (interest, no fee) or negative (fee, no interest), so the two never coincide within one close. The capitalized credit at end of Day 6 is the **sum of the rounded daily accruals** (which is why criterion 8's "discard the remainder" is incoherent — the total is *defined as* that sum; see REJECTED).
